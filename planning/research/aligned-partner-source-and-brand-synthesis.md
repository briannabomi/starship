# Aligned Partner Quiz: Source and Brand Synthesis

Date: 2026-07-15

## Purpose

This document translates the supplied Sacred Audit workbook and Bri's WorldCode source documents into implementation constraints for a cold-audience lead-magnet quiz. It is a source synthesis, not a validation study. It should be used alongside the cited UX, assessment-ethics, and relationship-science research in this directory.

## Product intent

The experience should help a cold follower name what they want, what they do not want, and where partnership mismatch may be creating friction. It should produce a useful reflection profile without claiming to predict compatibility, diagnose a relationship, or identify a single "perfect" partner.

The core brand promise is clarity without self-abandonment: a direct but humane mirror that turns implicit desires into language a person can use in real conversations.

## Audience and resistance profile

The primary audience is a high-performing, introspection-literate leader who can explain their patterns but struggles to act on what they know. They value speed, structure, discernment, privacy, and an integration of grounded evidence with spiritual language. They resist experiences that feel generic, overly clinical, manipulative, or like another long self-improvement assignment.

Design implications:

- Make the first useful action immediate; do not open with a long biography or heavy sales pitch.
- Use plain, decisive language and short prompts. Let optional helper text carry nuance.
- Respect sophistication without assuming the person has already decided on monogamy, marriage, gender roles, children, or a conventional household.
- Reflect answers back as a map and conversation starter, not a verdict.
- Keep spiritual language light in the quiz itself and more visible in the optional invitation after results.
- Never imply that taking the quiz, disclosing intimate information, or booking a session proves courage, worth, or readiness.

## Workbook question map

The ten workbook prompts are valuable as content seeds, but several should be adapted from open-ended journaling into low-friction, independently scored dimensions.

1. **Core values in partnership** - preserve as a priority-selection exercise; cap selections so tradeoffs are meaningful.
2. **Desired partnership type** - present inclusive, non-hierarchical options plus "exploring" and self-description. Do not score one structure as more aligned than another.
3. **Desired qualities / non-negotiables** - split preferences from true requirements; do not turn a long ideal-partner list into a perfection score.
4. **Deal-breakers** - frame as boundaries and incompatibilities. Separate safety violations from lifestyle preferences and never trivialize abuse by placing it beside food preferences.
5. **Repeating dating patterns** - use reflective pattern options plus "none/not sure." Avoid blame and deterministic attachment labels.
6. **Fantasy versus seeing clearly** - translate into observable decision habits, such as prioritizing potential, chemistry, consistency, or shared direction.
7. **Feeling appreciated** - avoid presenting the Five Love Languages as validated personality types. Ask directly about preferred expressions of care and support.
8. **How I want to provide** - ask about contribution and responsibility preferences without gendered or compulsory-provider assumptions.
9. **Adventure and identity** - measure desired balance between shared experiences and protected individual identity.
10. **Fear of being known** - make optional or use broad disclosure-comfort choices. Do not require a cold lead to type a sensitive secret.

## Recommended result architecture

Use a multi-dimensional profile rather than a soulmate type or compatibility percentage. The result should contain:

- a memorable orientation title that describes a current priority, not a fixed identity;
- top partnership values;
- desired relationship container or current exploration state;
- needs for intimacy, appreciation, autonomy, adventure, and shared responsibility;
- clearly separated requirements, preferences, and deal-breakers;
- one likely friction pattern phrased tentatively;
- three conversation prompts and one small next step;
- a reminder that real compatibility is co-created through consent, communication, responsiveness, and behavior over time.

Good title territory includes "Devoted Co-Creator," "Sovereign Explorer," "Grounded Builder," and "Depth Seeker," provided each is described as an orientation and paired with the dimensional profile. Avoid titles that rank people, imply pathology, or prescribe relationship structure.

## Voice guide

Desired voice:

- direct, intimate, discerning, and warm;
- spiritually literate but operationally clear;
- willing to name avoidance without shaming it;
- confident enough to use short sentences and white space;
- focused on power, desire, truth, choice, and self-trust.

Use phrases such as:

- "Name what is true for you now."
- "Clarity is not a contract. You are allowed to evolve."
- "Your result is a mirror, not a verdict."
- "The relationship you want needs language."
- "Choose the answer that feels true, not impressive."

Avoid source-workbook phrases that create unnecessary pressure or unsupported certainty, including claims that misaligned values inevitably make a relationship crack, that a person is "attracting" the wrong partner because of unclear intent, or that one answer exposes a hidden truth. Avoid "be brutally honest," "no exceptions," and similar coercive framing in sensitive moments.

## Sex and intimacy framing

The transcript names loss of sex and partner mismatch as central pain points. The quiz should address this directly but carefully:

- Ask about desired roles of sex, erotic connection, affection, and nonsexual intimacy independently.
- Include a private/skip option and avoid collecting explicit narratives.
- Acknowledge that desire varies across people and across time; mismatch is a conversation signal, not proof of failure.
- Do not imply that frequent sex defines a healthy relationship or that lack of sex has one cause.
- Include consent, safety, health, stress, caregiving, disability, medication, and life-stage context in result guidance without diagnosing any of them.

## Lead magnet and invitation

The result must stand alone as valuable. Email capture should be transparent and should not conceal the headline result. If email is requested, explain exactly what will be sent, make marketing consent distinct where required, and provide a clear path to continue without subscribing.

The post-result invitation can offer a deeper Alignment Strategy Session. It should describe the practical outcome of the conversation and identify spiritual guidance as part of Bri's approach without promising supernatural certainty, guaranteed relationship outcomes, or insight unavailable through the participant's own agency.
