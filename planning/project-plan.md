# Starship Project Plan

## Current Status

Starship is in discovery and planning. The first job is to turn the existing WorldCode context into a coherent project plan that future agents can extend without losing the voice, audience, and worldview.

## North Star

Build a project that helps high-performing leaders stop outsourcing authority, discern the soul signal from fear/protection/ego, and choose from the right part in ordinary life.

## Early Definition

Starship is not yet a finalized product, funnel, content system, or offer. It is the planning container for developing one.

The strongest current strategic direction is a transformation container or offer built around:

- discerning which part is driving a decision
- creating a felt relocation from mind/protector into soul signal
- integrating breakthroughs into daily choosing
- helping leaders hold more power without self-abandonment

## Source-Aligned Positioning

Primary positioning:

> I help leaders move from fearing their power to becoming a vessel for it.

Supporting frame:

> You are not confused about what you want. You are confused about which part of you is doing the wanting.

Audience:

High-performing founders, executives, and creative leaders who have already done therapy, coaching, plant medicine, and personal development work, but still cannot reliably act from what they know.

Core enemy:

Victim mentality and the broader seeking loop that keeps people outsourcing authority to methods, teachers, ceremonies, frameworks, or external validation.

## Planning Phases

### Phase 1: Foundation

- Preserve source context in the repo.
- Create an agent brief.
- Synthesize the core worldview, audience, offer constraints, and language.
- Identify what decisions still need user input.

### Phase 2: Strategy

- Define what Starship is: offer, content engine, curriculum, operating system, community, app, or hybrid.
- Choose the primary buyer journey.
- Define the transformation promise using the offer framework.
- Decide the first deliverable format and minimum viable scope.

### Phase 3: Offer Architecture

- Build the offer using the OFFERS framework:
  - Outcome
  - Fit
  - Fast
  - Edge
  - Risk Reversal
  - Stack
- Name the mechanism and phases.
- Engineer a Day-7 win.
- Create proof and measurement strategy.

### Phase 4: Experience Design

- Map the client journey.
- Define rituals, practices, checkpoints, and integration structures.
- Decide what is live, async, written, audio, video, AI-assisted, or done-for-you.
- Create agent-ready specs for content, curriculum, operations, and assets.

### Phase 5: Build Plan

- Convert the strategy into implementation tasks.
- Assign workstreams.
- Maintain the repo as the central memory for all agents.

## Immediate Next Decisions

- What is Starship meant to become first: an offer, a curriculum, a content strategy, a software-assisted experience, a brand world, or a full business operating plan?
- Who is the first user/buyer: Bri herself, clients, internal agents, or the public audience?
- What is the first shippable artifact: offer map, sales page, curriculum, content calendar, onboarding flow, or project spec?
- What constraints matter now: timeline, launch date, budget, team, tech stack, or audience channel?
